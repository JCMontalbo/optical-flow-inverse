# Downstream experiment: does flow-based augmentation help a segmenter with scarce labels?

*Pre-registered plan. Written before any training run; results will be reported against it whether or not
they come out in favour.*

## Question

The dissertation's motivating claim (ch. 1): synthetic images generated by transforming a recovered flow
field respect the geometry of the scene, so they should be better training data than blind rotate / skew /
shear augmentation when labels are scarce. The dissertation did not have time to test this. This experiment
does, on a task where the (image, label) pairs generated by `ofi.augment` + `ofi.labels` can be used directly.

**H1.** With few labelled frames, a segmenter trained with flow-generated (image, label) pairs scores higher
than one trained with standard geometric augmentation.
**H2.** The gain comes from the *perturbations* (§3.1, §3.2, §4.5), not merely from propagating labels to
neighbouring real frames (which is a strong but different idea).
**H3.** The gain shrinks as labels become plentiful.

## Data

**DAVIS 2016** (50 videos, 3,455 frames, one binary object mask per frame, 480p; CC BY-NC 4.0).
Standard split: 30 train videos, 20 val videos. Chosen because it is exactly the setting the video scripts
already handle — real frames, real motion, a real mask on every frame — and because the standard metric
(J-mean, i.e. mean IoU on val) is well known, so the number means something to a reader.

Frames resized to 256 × 448 (aspect preserved). Flow is estimated on greyscale and applied to all three
colour channels and to the mask.

*Alternative if fidelity to the medical setting is preferred:* MSD Task02 Heart (20 cardiac MRI volumes,
adjacent slices as the frame pair, left-atrium mask). Same design, slice pairs instead of frame pairs. Smaller
and noisier; the DAVIS result is the one to run first.

## Design

**Label budget** — labelled frames per training video: **1, 2, 5, 20** (≈ 30, 60, 150, 600 labelled frames in
total; the remaining frames of the training videos are available *unlabelled*, as in practice).
Labelled frames are chosen evenly spaced within each video, same choice for every arm and seed.

**Arms** (each trained from scratch on the same labelled frames):

| arm | training data | what it tests |
|---|---|---|
| A `none` | labelled frames only | floor |
| B `standard` | A + random flip, rotation ±15°, scale 0.8–1.2, shear ±10°, brightness/contrast jitter, applied to image and mask on the fly | the blind augmentation the dissertation criticises |
| C `flow` | A + **M = 8** generated pairs per labelled frame: flow to neighbours k±1, k±2; §3.1 homotopy ε ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75}, §3.2 localised windows (2 random), §4.5 windowed rotation / squeeze / shear at the point of largest camera-relative motion; image and mask carried together | the dissertation's method |
| D `standard+flow` | B applied on top of C | whether they compose |
| E `propagate` | A + labels carried to the 4 real neighbours k±1, k±2 (no perturbation) | control for H2: is it the perturbation or just more real-ish frames? |

Generated pairs are produced once, offline, and cached, so every seed sees the same synthetic set.
Occlusion handling: none (the dissertation has none); its cost is part of the result.

**Model** — a small U-Net (4 levels, 16-32-64-128 channels, ≈ 0.5 M parameters), BCE + Dice loss, Adam
1e-3 with cosine decay, batch 8, **40 epochs or 4,000 steps, whichever is first**, early-stopping on a
held-out slice of the *training* videos' unlabelled-but-actually-labelled frames (never on val).
Same recipe for every arm; no per-arm tuning.

**Seeds** — 3 per cell. 4 budgets × 5 arms × 3 seeds = **60 runs**. On the RTX 4080 each run is ≈ 3–6 min
at this size, so the full grid is ≈ 4–6 h, overnight-able. Flow estimation for the augmentation
(≈ 600 labelled frames × 4 neighbours at 448 px, ≈ 8 s each in NumPy) is ≈ 1.3 h, cached.

**Metric** — J-mean (mean IoU) over all frames of the 20 val videos, reported as mean ± std over seeds.
Secondary: J-mean at the lowest budget only, per video, to see where flow augmentation helps and hurts.

## Pre-registered success criteria

- **H1 supported** if, at 1 and 2 labels/video, arm C (or D) beats arm B by ≥ 2 J-mean points with
  non-overlapping seed ranges. Between 0 and 2 points: "consistent with a small effect"; ≤ 0: not supported.
- **H2 supported** if arm C beats arm E by ≥ 1 point at those budgets. If E ≈ C, the honest conclusion is
  that label propagation, not perturbation, is doing the work.
- **H3** is descriptive: the C − B gap at 20 labels/video vs. at 1.
- Any outcome is reported. A null result is still a result the dissertation could not obtain.

## Amendments made before the first training run

Recorded here because they change the design above; all were decided after looking at generated
*samples*, before any training result existed.

1. **Neighbours k±4 instead of k±1, k±2.** Adjacent DAVIS frames move ~1.4 px, so the homotopy variants
   were nearly identical to the real frame and arm C would have been arm A in disguise. Flows are now
   estimated to k±4 (≈5–6 px) and k±1, all PSNR-gated at 25 dB; arm C uses the farthest accepted
   neighbour, arm E uses every accepted one (up to 4 real neighbours, not 4 from k±1, k±2).
2. **Stronger perturbations:** window gain 3 (was 2), area-preserving amplitude 6 % of height (was 4 %).
3. **Every cell trains for exactly 4,000 steps** (the "or 40 epochs" cap would have given the 1-label
   budget 120 steps). Early stopping still selects the checkpoint.
4. The U-Net as built has 1.9 M parameters (widths 16-32-64-128 with a 256-wide bottleneck), not 0.5 M.
5. bf16 autocast and cuDNN autotuning for speed; no effect on the design.

## Risks and what would be done about them

| risk | mitigation |
|---|---|
| Horn–Schunck flow too poor on fast DAVIS motion, generated frames garbage | use only k±1, k±2 (small motion); pyramid with 5 levels at 448 px; drop generated pairs whose reconstruction PSNR of the real neighbour is < 25 dB, and report how many were dropped |
| C ≈ E (perturbations add nothing over neighbours) | that is what H2 is for; report it plainly |
| Standard augmentation is a strong baseline and wins | likely at 20 labels/video; the interesting regime is 1–2 |
| Generated pairs dominate the training set (8:1) | sample real and generated with equal probability per batch |
| DAVIS is CC BY-NC | fine for a research repo; no redistribution of frames beyond a few figure crops with attribution |
